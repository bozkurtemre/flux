Agents

Hand it to Claude Code.

Flux is an MCP server. Turn it on from the status bar, paste one command into a terminal, and an agent reads your logs with Flux's own tools — searching, counting, opening a query in front of you. Loopback only, behind a token, read-only.

How it works

Connect, explore, act.

Three minutes from download to the first spike explained. Nothing to deploy, nothing to run on the server.

1

Connect a source

A Graylog URL and token, Elasticsearch through Kibana, or a folder of local log files and Docker containers. The token stays on your Mac.

2

Explore

Streams and endpoints appear as a tree, with request counts and error rates. Open one, type Lucene, press ⌘ ⏎. Save what you will need again.

3

Act

Explain the spike, trace the id, investigate to the fix, watch the query, hand the case to Claude Code — and ship.

Privacy

Your logs never leave your Mac.

Flux talks to your sources directly. There is no server of ours in between, and nothing of what you read is sent anywhere.

Tokens stay local

Connections and their tokens live in one file on your Mac. They are never synced, exported or put in a link.

Read-only

Flux only searches and aggregates. It never writes to Graylog, Kibana or Elasticsearch, and neither can an agent through it.

Only preferences sync

Sign in and your appearance and keymap follow you to every Mac. Queries, projects and log content do not.

Open source

Apache 2.0, on GitHub. Read how it keeps your data, or build it yourself.
