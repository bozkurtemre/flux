10:44One request, every service

One request, every service

Take the id from the failing line. ⇧⌘I searches it as text in every stream of the connection at once and tells its story oldest first: a lane per stream, a dot per line, the failure in red. Click a dot to read the line. The same ids, logged between your services, draw the service map — no agent, no tracing library, nothing to install on the server.

10:46The case

The case

Right-click the line and choose Investigate. Flux gathers the request across services, how often it happened and who else it hit, the line of code that logged it through the project's folder, and the commit that last changed that line. Then it hands the whole case to Claude Code in the terminal — one click.

10:51Handed to the terminal

Handed to the terminal

The case lands in the terminal where Claude Code runs, with a brief: find the cause, fix it on a branch with a test that fails without it, open a draft pull request. While it works, the agent reads your logs back through Flux — Flux is an MCP server with eight tools that read your sources and never write to them, listening on 127.0.0.1 behind a token it makes for you. One line adds it to Claude Code.

  1. 1

    Turn it on — click the MCP icon in the status bar and tick Enable MCP Server.

    acme POST /v1/charges 1,244 rows · 214 ms demo data
  2. 2

    Add it to Claude Code —

    claude mcp add --transport http flux http://127.0.0.1:4319/mcp --header "Authorization: Bearer …"
  3. 3

    Ask — claude, then a question.

The run above is made up; the tools and their arguments are real. The agent's reasoning is the agent's, not Flux's. Loopback only, behind a token, and nothing is ever written to a source.

10:58Keep an eye on it

Keep an eye on it

Save the query, right-click it, Watch for new matches. While Flux is open it asks the query every minute — or the interval you choose — and the island under the notch keeps the score over every app: new matches, a sound if you like, a row you can click. Turn it around with Alert when › Nothing arrives, for a cron or a webhook that goes quiet. And when the fix merges, the island watches the ten minutes after it.

11:02Where this ran

On your Mac, against your sources.

Flux talks to Graylog, Kibana or your files from your Mac. Nothing you read is sent anywhere, and there is no server of ours between you and your logs. Two things do reach us: a version check at launch and every hour, and — once you sign in — your appearance, keymap, console and grid settings, so they follow you to another Mac.
